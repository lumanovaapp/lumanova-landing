# Conversion & Retention — The Playbook

Deep reference for steps 4 ("retention") and 5 ("conversion") of the workflow.
Design decisions here move the actual business metric: keep visitors engaged and
guide them to the purchase/signup.

## 1. The conversion funnel mindset

Every page has ONE job: move the visitor to the next step. Define that step
before designing. The macro-flow is Attention → Interest → Desire → Trust →
Action. Each section of the page should advance one of these and hand off to the
next. If a section doesn't move the visitor forward, it's decoration — cut or
demote it.

**Design for one primary conversion goal per page.** Competing goals (subscribe
*and* buy *and* book a demo, all equal weight) split attention and lower all of
them. Choose the primary action; make everything else secondary.

## 2. Hero anatomy (above the fold)

The hero decides whether they stay. It must answer, fast:
- **What is this?** (category clarity)
- **What do I get?** (outcome, not feature list)
- **Who's it for?** (self-identification)
- **What do I do?** (one obvious primary CTA)

Structure that reliably works:
- **Eyebrow** (optional small label — category or audience).
- **Headline:** the core outcome/promise, specific. Lead with the transformation
  or result, not the mechanism.
- **Subhead:** one or two lines expanding the how / removing the main objection.
- **Primary CTA** + optional secondary (lower emphasis). Add a risk-reducer under
  it ("Free • no card required").
- **Proof strip:** logos, rating, or a hard number, immediately establishing
  credibility.
- **Visual:** a real product shot / demo, not an abstract graphic — it shows,
  don't-tell what they'll get.

Keep the hero uncluttered — one dominant message, one dominant action.

## 3. Retention & scroll design

- **5-second test:** if a stranger can't state what it is and what to do after 5
  seconds, the hero fails — fix that before anything below.
- **One focal point per viewport:** as they scroll, each screenful presents a
  single clear priority. Use size, weight, color, and space to point at it.
- **Scannability:** subheads carry the argument on their own; a skimmer reading
  only headings should still get the pitch. Short paragraphs, one idea per block,
  emphasized key phrases.
- **Scroll momentum / open loops:** end sections on a small curiosity gap; let the
  next section peek above the fold so there's always a visual reason to continue.
- **Progressive disclosure:** reveal detail on demand (accordions, "learn more,"
  tabs) instead of dumping everything at once — respects cognitive load.
- **Perceived performance:** skeletons, instant interaction feedback, optimistic
  UI. A product that *feels* fast retains better and reads premium.
- **Reduce choices (Hick's Law):** fewer nav links and CTAs = faster, more
  confident decisions.

## 4. CTA craft

- **One primary action, repeated** down the page at natural decision points
  (after the hero, after key proof, after pricing, at the end). Don't make users
  scroll back to act.
- **Visual dominance (Von Restorff):** the primary CTA is the most distinct
  element in its view — accent fill, high contrast, clear affordance. Secondary
  actions are visibly quieter.
- **Copy = action + outcome/value:** "Start my free trial," "Get the toolkit,"
  "See it in action" beat "Submit," "Click here," "Sign up." First person ("Start
  *my* trial") often lifts clicks.
- **Directional cues:** whitespace, contrast, arrows, and human gaze in imagery
  should point toward the CTA.
- **Risk reducers** adjacent to the button: guarantee, "no card," "cancel
  anytime," "2-minute setup."

## 5. Social proof & trust

- **Adjacency:** place proof next to the claim it supports and next to CTAs — at
  the decision moment, not siloed in one distant section.
- **Types, strongest first:** quantified results ("cut hiring time 60%") >
  named testimonials with face/role/company > recognizable logos > ratings/counts
  > press. Specific and attributed beats vague and anonymous.
- **Trust signals:** money-back guarantee, security/payment badges at checkout,
  transparent pricing, real company info, clear privacy stance. Cluster them
  where hesitation peaks (pricing, forms, checkout).

## 6. Pricing psychology & tables

- **Anchor:** show a higher-value or crossed-out original first so the target
  price feels reasonable by comparison.
- **Highlight the recommended tier:** visually elevate one plan (border, badge,
  scale, "Most popular") to guide the default choice and reduce paralysis.
- **~3 tiers:** too many options stall decisions (Hick's Law); 3 is a strong
  default (good/better/best), occasionally a 4th enterprise.
- **Framing:** monthly framing of annual cost, per-seat/per-use where it looks
  smaller, "less than X" comparisons, annual-discount toggle with savings shown.
- **Value clarity:** lead each tier with the outcome/who-it's-for, then features.
  Make differences scannable; align feature rows; use tabular numerals.
- **Reduce end-of-funnel risk:** guarantee and "no card required" right in/near
  the table.

## 7. Objection handling

Every non-buyer has an unspoken "but…". Surface and dissolve the top ones on the
page:
- **Comparison** vs alternatives / the status quo (why now, why you).
- **FAQ** targeting real objections (price, effort, risk, fit, switching cost) —
  not filler questions.
- **"What you get"** concreteness to counter "is it worth it?"
- **Guarantee / free tier / demo** to counter risk.
Position each objection-handler right before or beside the relevant CTA so it
clears the path at the decision point.

## 8. Form & checkout friction

Friction is where conversions leak. Design it out:
- **Fewer fields.** Every field drops completion — ask only what's needed now;
  defer the rest. Prefer email-only / SSO to start.
- **Don't gate value behind signup** prematurely; let people experience worth
  first where possible.
- **One column, labels above fields, logical order,** clear inline validation,
  visible progress on multi-step, obvious primary action.
- **Checkout:** minimize steps and surprises. Show total early, offer guest
  checkout, express-pay options, trust badges at the payment step, and never
  spring unexpected costs late (the top cart-abandon cause).

## 9. E-commerce / Shopify specifics

- **Product page = mini landing page:** strong gallery (multiple angles, zoom,
  lifestyle + on-white), benefit-led title, clear price, prominent single "Add to
  cart," concise benefit copy, reviews near the buy button, shipping/returns
  clarity, trust badges.
- **Imagery is the product** online — consistent, high-quality, well-lit shots
  with unified treatment do the heaviest conversion lifting.
- **Reduce cart abandonment:** transparent total cost early, guest checkout,
  multiple/express payment methods, progress indication, reassurance at payment.
- **Merchandising & momentum:** clear category nav, strong PLP hierarchy, honest
  scarcity/social proof ("low stock," "N sold"), thoughtful cross-sell that helps
  rather than distracts.
- **Mobile-first:** most storefront traffic is mobile — thumb-reachable buy
  button, fast images, sticky add-to-cart.

## 10. Ethics of persuasion

Use psychology to *help the right visitor decide confidently*, never to trick.
Avoid dark patterns — fake scarcity/countdowns, hidden costs, forced continuity,
confusing opt-outs, guilt-y decline copy. They spike short-term numbers and
destroy trust, refunds, and retention. Honest clarity converts better over any
horizon that matters and protects the brand you're trying to build.
